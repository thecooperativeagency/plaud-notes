Meeting Notes

Improving Service Sales Performance

  • [Speaker 1] highlighted a recent new car sale that took two days and yielded only $1,500 profit, emphasizing the effort required per sale and drawing a parallel to the dedication needed from service writers.
  • From April to July, average hours per repair order (RO) was 2.44. After implementing callbacks for lost opportunities and offering discounts, the average increased to 2.69 hours per RO over the last two months.
  • New target for customer-pay work is 3.0 hours per RO, considered achievable.
  • Writers should focus on selling necessary repairs while customers are on-site; 0% financing options should be promoted to help close service sales.
  • The dealership's warranty claims are lower than the average dealer, possibly due to being overly cautious — valid warranty work, particularly related to the differential, may be missed.
  • Service advisors need to conduct better customer interviews to identify and document vehicle issues properly, including prompting customers about specific symptoms (e.g., vibrations, rubbing sensations on turns).

F&I Performance Review

  • Significant improvement last month: F&I reached $2,600 per copy with a 64% finance penetration rate.
  • Discussion held about reintroducing a "finance fifth" on used cars to push toward a $3,000 per copy target.

Vehicle Differential Service and Warranty Issues

  • A recurring and systemic issue exists with the differential on Q5 models. Cars with the problem are being traded in without the issue being addressed under warranty.

    • - The repair involves a differential flush (~$600); if unsuccessful, full replacement costs ~$2,500, often absorbed by the dealership. - A specific Q5 has been in service for a year and a half without resolution; a new module is expected but no contingency plan exists if that fails.

  • Technicians are reportedly reluctant to perform the warranty diff flush because it is time-consuming relative to compensation.
  • Manufacturer policy requires a customer complaint before warranty work can be initiated, creating tension with the dealership's goal of proactive customer retention.
  • The issue is a known TSB, but the manufacturer classifies it as a "feel" concern rather than a safety issue to avoid a recall.
  • Under warranty, diff and motor mount issues cannot be directly upsold; the approach is to inform customers, add complaint lines, and manage through warranty processes.
  • The team agreed this puts both the dealership and customers in a poor position and that the manufacturer should bear responsibility.
  • Plan: Discuss directly with manufacturer representatives (Sam and Mark) during their visit on October 27th, and separately schedule a meeting with representative Terrence to address the Q5 differential issue and broader service/warranty policies.

    • ---

Internal Costs and Inter-Departmental Trust

  • Significant conflict exists between the used car department and service department regarding repair costs on trade-in vehicles.

    • - Used car department feels "jammed up" by high service costs that erode profit margins. - Example: A $500 price discrepancy for TPM sensors between their service department and an external Lincoln dealer. - Used car manager stated they will send work elsewhere if it is more cost-effective.

  • There is widespread distrust of service technician quotes for used cars — estimates are perceived as inflated or unnecessary (e.g., $5,000–$6,000 quotes on cars bought for $17,000–$20,000).

    • - Specific concerns: charging diagnostic fees when the issue is already known, quoting major repairs based on poor-quality photos, and charging $189 to plug in a car to charge its battery. - Technicians are perceived as possibly pressured by management to maximize revenue per job.

  • The team indicated greater trust in independent shops than their own service department.
  • No clear resolution or collaboration plan between the two departments was established.

Diagnostic (Diag) Charges and Integration with Repair Quotes

  • Diag charges apply when work requires disassembly beyond initial inspection (e.g., door/window fault testing, dye for oil leak detection).
  • If diag requires disassembly, related repairs should not be charged separately — diag should be rolled into the repair cost.
  • Agreed principle: diag charges should be credited against repair costs; questionable cases will be reviewed on request.
  • Clear thresholds for when diag charges are applied and how they are credited have not yet been formally documented.

UCI Inspection Scope, Timing, and Transparency

  • UCI uses a checklist with green/yellow/red statuses covering: brakes, tires (tread depth), leaks, lights, belts, fluids, A/C function, and traction control.
  • Inspection duration varies by vehicle and mileage.
  • A request was made for a fully documented, transparent checklist of all UCI items.
  • Concern raised about short inspections being billed at higher hours — no benchmarks by vehicle type have been established.
  • Action: Share the itemized UCI checklist with the team.

AI Sales Assistant Issues

  • The current AI configuration is not working effectively; its benefit to sales is unclear and the verbiage is considered subpar.
  • Key problems:

    • - AI stops communicating with a customer indefinitely once a salesperson engages. - AI cannot be easily re-engaged after a set period of salesperson inactivity (e.g., customer leaves without buying). - The AI and sales team use two different phone numbers for texting, risking customer confusion — no decision was made on whether to pursue a single-number solution. - Concern that the AI may be "pestering" customers and negatively impacting the closing ratio, though no definitive measurement method was established.

  • Team discussed needing a "pause" button that remains active until manually un-paused, and the ability to set custom statuses (e.g., hot, cold) to control AI interaction.
  • Lance has meetings scheduled for October 5th and 6th, 2026 to address AI configuration issues with the provider.
