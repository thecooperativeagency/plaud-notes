Core Synopsis

The business is under dual pressure: service revenue is missing daily targets and leadership scrutiny is about to intensify. Operationally, stores are meeting or exceeding some front-end retail metrics, but customer-pay service throughput and dollar volume are underperforming despite high labor costs and nine techs on the floor. Politically, Open Roads hired a seasoned COO (ex-Lithia M&A) who will run a forensic review, while ownership has discovered absentee executive leadership, heightening the risk of top-down intervention. Evidence points to a capacity-and-culture problem in Service: an underperforming, change-resistant shop foreman, talent scarcity in Jackson, and insufficient sales/service advisor horsepower are constraining RO count and average dollars per RO. Advisory support is weak; field reps flag gaps but provide “sell more parts” guidance without process repairs. Meanwhile, the team is rationalizing spend (cut Cars.com and Automotive Mastermind), experimenting with Podium and AI call-routing to avoid adding headcount, and planning an internal newsletter to boost engagement. We are accountable for reversing service underperformance before the COO’s audit converts soft misses (−$81k YTD bonuses) into hard structural mandates; we commit to a focused reset on technician productivity, advisor effectiveness, and leadership accountability in the shop.

Service Revenue Turnaround: From Fragmented Effort to Focused System

  • The Constraints Driving the Shortfall (Diagnostic Deep-Dive)

    • - Phenomenon: Customer-pay workshop sales must hit ~$7.5k/day net but are missing, despite nine technicians. - Mechanism: Low technician productivity and/or under-optimized dispatch; inadequate service advisor selling (ARO, MPIs, menuing); leadership blockage from a 30-year shop foreman resisting new process; recruiting constraints in Jackson shrink the quality talent pool. - Root Causes: - Process: Inconsistent RO build depth (inspections, fluid exchanges, maintenance menus) and weak parts/service coordination for same-day approvals. - People: Advisor counts and closing skills are insufficient to lift CP ARO; shop foreman undercuts change adoption; billable hours per tech likely below target due to dispatch/load leveling gaps and comeback/hold times. - Tools: Phone triage and routing improved with Podium AI pilot, but service scheduling, MPI photo/video usage, and follow-up may lack rigor; vendor “advice” is informational, not operational.

  • The Near-Term Audit Reality (Challenge-Response)

    • - Status Quo: YTD missed bonuses of ~$81k; other stores (e.g., “battle lines” at 105%) are outperforming. - Imminent Gap: New COO Scott Cusick will drill into CP revenue, labor efficiency, ARO, effective labor rate (ELR), parts-to-labor ratios, and advisor close rates. - Response: Preempt the audit by installing a visible playbook—daily RO targets by advisor, hours per RO goals by tech, foreman accountability, and same-day upsell cadence—so the narrative shifts from excuses to measurable recovery.

  • Leadership and Culture Reset in the Shop (Dialectical Analysis)

    • - Thesis: Long-tenured foreman stability provides tribal knowledge. - Antithesis: “We’ve always done it this way” blocks throughput, modern MPI-driven selling, and dispatch optimization. - Synthesis: Retain tribal knowledge but remove the veto power—reassign or replace the foreman role if KPIs slip; empower a lead willing to run load balancing, enforce MPIs, and coach techs on billed hours targets.

  • Capacity and Mix: Advisors, Techs, and the Phone Front Door (Structural Assembly)

    • - Technicians: Nine techs require strong dispatching and steady feeder work. Without advisor-driven approvals, tech idle time rises and CP falls. - Service Advisors: Add or upskill to lift ARO via rigorous MPIs, photo/video transparency, menu presentations, and same-day decisioning scripts. - Phone and Scheduling: Podium AI call-routing trial is a cost-efficient hedge; ROI depends on reducing abandoned calls, accelerating appointment setting, and routing to the right advisor without extra headcount.

  • Vendor and Spend Rationalization (Trade-Offs with Teeth)

    • - Cuts: Cars.com and Automotive Mastermind removed; ad spend normalized to ~$25k after credits; Podium added for service communications and AI phone support. - Trade-off: Lower third-party lead spend demands higher conversion of in-house traffic; service must offset with process-driven ARO and ELR gains.

  • Internal Communication and Morale Levers

    • - Newsletter/Magazine: Use “Employee of the Month” and role spotlights (e.g., new parts manager Shelley) to reinforce desired behaviors and retention amid market hiring challenges.
