09-01 Analysis of Marketing Performance and Strategic Realignment

This conversation documents the termination of Lance's marketing contract due to performance issues, primarily concerning social media and inventory photography. The discussion transitions to a strategic analysis of the dealership's marketing needs, a demonstration of an advanced analytics tool developed by Lance, and the negotiation of a new, consulting-based role for him, culminating in a handover plan for his replacement, Thomas. ------------

Preliminary Marketing Discussions

The dialogue begins with several operational inquiries before shifting to the main topic of performance. Speaker 2 asks Lance to identify the supplier for previously successful LSU and Southern-themed shirts, which featured "Tigers" and "Jaguars" on the back, with the intent to reorder them. The conversation then turns to an email regarding marketing strategy, where Lance clarifies that while keywords and minor campaigns are running, there is no significant extra spend allocated. He mentions that his Jackson store spends approximately $2,200 on a service marketing program called "Service Combat" but questions if this store needs to invest the full $2,200-$3,000. Speaker 3 interjects, stating the client is interested in buying more keywords and social media services, leading to a brief discussion about Porsche service clinics, for which Lance notes that historical data is available. ------------

Performance Review and Termination of Contract

The conversation takes a critical turn as Speaker 3 directly informs Lance that a new employee, Thomas, has been hired to replace him. Speaker 3 outlines the reasons for this decision, stating that Lance appears "stretched too thin" across too many accounts, leading to neglect of key priorities. The specific areas of underperformance cited include inconsistent inventory photography and poor social media content, which is characterized as having "zero engagement" and appearing to be just one photo cropped multiple times. Lance defends his photography, explaining his process involves shooting "wides, mediums, and tights" rather than cropping, and that his primary focus has been on higher-level SEO, SEM, and CRM cleanup. Despite his defense, Lance accepts the feedback and the termination, acknowledging that the store's needs for a constant, on-site presence—a "daily guy"—are not aligned with his consulting-focused specialty. ------------

Transition Planning and Negotiation of a New Role

Following the decision to terminate the current contract, the discussion shifts to organizing a professional transition. Lance agrees to a one-month notice period, during which he will submit one final bill and ensure a complete and smooth handover of responsibilities to his replacement, Thomas. He commits to personally guiding Thomas through all account logins and operational processes. Simultaneously, Lance proposes a new, restructured relationship where he would serve in a high-level consulting capacity, focusing on strategic oversight, data analysis, and managing SEO/SEM vendors, rather than daily content creation. Speaker 3 is receptive to this idea, and they agree to determine an appropriate fee for this new arrangement. A key condition is established: Thomas will be prohibited from working for any competing Porsche dealerships, a term Lance agrees is standard. ------------

Demonstration of Advanced Marketing Analytics Tool

To underscore his value as a high-level strategist, Lance presents a custom analytics dashboard he developed. He explains that the tool integrates disparate data sources—including website traffic from GA4, CRM lead data from systems like VinSolutions, and live inventory data—into a single, coherent view. He demonstrates its capabilities, showing how it visualizes traffic sources, inventory aging, new/used/CPO splits, and demand shape by model. A key feature highlighted is its ability to de-duplicate lead sources, providing a true picture of retail opportunities by filtering out low-quality leads from buying centers like KBB. Lance uses examples from other dealerships to show how the dashboard can reveal trade-in patterns, sales performance by zip code, and critical operational metrics like lead response times, positioning the tool as a way to be a "guardian of spend" by focusing on improving closing ratios before increasing marketing budgets. ------------

Strategic Discussion on Dealership Marketing and Operations

The analytics demonstration sparks a broader strategic conversation about the nuances of automotive marketing. The speakers contrast the high-intensity, campaign-driven needs of brands like BMW and Audi with the more brand-insulated market of Porsche, which doesn't require the same level of aggressive marketing overhauls. They discuss the significant, often underestimated, time and expense required for quality content creation, noting a short video can take days to produce. The conversation also touches on operational efficiencies, such as the high cost ($120,000) and effectiveness of "light box" photo booths for consistent vehicle imaging and automated service-lane inspection systems that can significantly raise the hours per repair order (RO) by instantly identifying service needs. They conclude that in a market with product pipeline challenges, like the upcoming shift of the gas-powered Macan to a new model, having a strong service operation and skilled advisors is paramount. ------------
