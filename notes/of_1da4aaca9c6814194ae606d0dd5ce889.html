Consult: Virtual Contact Assistant Pause Controls and Follow-up

id of_1da4aaca9c6814194ae606d0dd5ce889spoken/infer: Gaige+Bo VCA pause/dashboard/virtual desk

[](plaud://image?type=summaryCard&id=oc_su_1da4aaca9c6814194ae606d0dd5ce889_i1)

Lance, Gage, and Bo reviewed Virtual Contact Assistant performance, pause behavior, dashboard access, and follow-up options for virtual deals. The immediate conclusion was not to create a new process yet. The stores can use timed pauses while Gage seeks a definitive answer on how VCA resumes after a customer changes processes.

VCA pause behavior remains under review

Gage asked whether a customer on the VCA pause list would resume AI follow-up after entering an unsold showroom-visit process. He had raised the question with developers during virtual contact assistant office hours and contacted a senior product manager. He did not yet have a definitive answer.

A test lead could show whether a paused customer re-engages after a showroom visit is opened and closed. Gage noted that this would not settle the larger question: whether the pause list stops everything or whether statuses and processes can return a customer to VCA engagement.

Lance later found that the pause control lets users choose how long to pause an agent. He said the sales team had treated the feature as if they lacked control, though the duration appears directly in the dropdown.

For a customer whose deal is being worked, Lance’s current approach is to pause VCA for seven days. If the vehicle is not sold, follow-up resumes after seven days. If the deal closes, VCA stops contacting the customer.

Dashboard access and performance tracking

The relevant Insights report is the Virtual Assistant Dashboard. It covers all three stores. Managers who already have reporting access should be able to view it because it is a default system dashboard.

Gage recommended that general managers monitor:

  • Text conversations: Review unique conversations and anything unanswered.
  • Negative sentiment: Treat it as a screening tool, not proof of a bad interaction. The system may flag comments such as “I bought a car already” or “not interested” as negative.
  • Missed appointments: Managers can identify appointments VCA set but customers missed, then try to re-engage those customers directly.

At Audi last month, the assistant set four appointments. It also appeared in 39 showroom visits and 13 sales totaling nearly $60,000 in gross. Gage stressed that the dashboard does not claim the assistant generated that revenue. It shows that it touched those sales in some way.

BMW’s assistant had already set six appointments this month. It set 10 last month and was projected to set 26 this month. Gage viewed that as evidence that the assistant improves as it learns and follows guardrails.

Gage said text should remain the main focus because email engagement is weak. Lance agreed that email performance has been a long-standing problem. He said AMP’s all-text approach can remove task creation, while FullPath is beginning to integrate with the CRM more effectively.

Dealer instructions and pricing rules

Lance completed and installed custom dealer instructions for all three stores. Porsche had been the only store with saved instructions before the update.

He built the instructions by collecting every prior Podium thumbs-up and thumbs-down correction. He also copied the “instant answers” into his agent, then had it draft store-specific instruction sets. The result is a detailed baseline that can be expanded as managers find responses they dislike.

Gage called the Porsche instruction set unusually detailed. He said this level of setup is what dealers need if they want AI to behave as expected. He described the product as evolving and said ongoing refinements will remain necessary as AI changes.

Pricing disclosure is enabled for Audi and Porsche. Lance said Porsche and BMW cannot negotiate new-vehicle prices. The assistant may discuss used-vehicle pricing. The option to include vehicle price in the first contact message remains off because Lance considers it unnecessary.

Virtual desk deals do not justify a new process yet

Lance asked whether the CRM already has a process for customers whose deals were desked remotely but who did not buy. He initially considered opening a showroom visit even when the customer had not entered the store.

Gage cautioned against using the showroom-visit process for virtual deals. Its follow-up language includes messages such as “thank you for stopping in” and “thank you for visiting the store.” That could send inaccurate messages after a remotely desked deal.

Gage outlined an alternative: a custom process for unclosed virtual desk deals, with VCA included. Staff would have to place customers into it manually because merely desking a deal would not trigger it automatically. This would avoid false showroom messaging, but it would require disciplined manual handling so no customer falls through the cracks.

They agreed no new process was needed at this point. Lance said the issue is mainly one of setting expectations with his team. A custom process can be reconsidered store by store if virtual phone and internet deals become frequent.

Muse and personal AI agents

Bo described Muse as a free AI assistant that runs in a separate server and browser. He said it can perform tasks such as booking, buying, shipping, negotiating, and working through Facebook and Instagram. He said dealers are using it to digest inventory, identify matching service customers, and make automated offers.

Lance said he uses Grok for some tasks but considers his Hermes agent much stronger than other agents he has tried. He was interested in whether Hermes could connect to Muse through an MCP or use Meta AI as a provider. Lance said Ada was being asked to see whether Muse could be connected.

Bo said Muse searched dealership listings, Cars.com, CarGurus, and AutoTrader for used vehicles within $3,000 of JD Power retail and within 50 miles of his ZIP code. He said it captured VINs, valued vehicles, and returned matches. He also said it processed more than 2,500 complex documents in a minute or two.

Bo said he created four businesses’ Facebook advertising setups over the weekend. For a lumberyard with kitchen and bath design, he said the tool pulled material from the company website, built and launched a form-filled Facebook campaign, and generated three new-home-build leads within about an hour after he put $100 behind it.

The group speculated that Muse’s free access may be intended to build adoption and collect data. Lance noted that Grok costs $17 a month. Bo said OpenAI had moved some capabilities to a $180-a-month premium tier.
