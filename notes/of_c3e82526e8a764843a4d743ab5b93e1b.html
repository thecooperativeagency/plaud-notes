This working session focuses on structuring and refining a multi-tiered personal training offer framework for an upcoming business course, navigating worksheet friction, branding decisions, and strategic delegation to AI tools. The participants evaluate delivery models ranging from high-ticket in-home concierge packages to scalable hybrid coaching, while also weighing service scaling against physical gym management opportunities.

---

Offer Ideation and Training Delivery Models

The discussion opens with an evaluation of existing marketable skills and documented client success milestones to establish a clear coaching value proposition. Identified informal and formal competencies include cooking guidance, mothering advice, and targeted strength training designed to correct physical imbalances. Client wins—specifically referenced through transformations such as Alondra, Michael McLean, and Michael Gooden gaining strength and confidence—serve as foundational social proof. A key differentiation strategy involves coaching runners on strength and conditioning specifically to enhance their running mechanics and overall athletic performance.

To productize the business, the participants explore three primary coaching delivery models:

  • Productized Programming Cycles: Converting training routines into distinct, sellable cycle packages (e.g., priced between $500 and $1,500) rather than open-ended time-for-money services.
  • Hybrid Coaching: Offering independent remote or local training where clients execute workouts on their own and sync for coaching support via weekly or bi-weekly phone and Zoom check-ins.
  • High-Ticket Concierge Service ("CCM" - Concierge Christine Marie): A premium white-glove offering targeting high-end clientele who have their own private home gyms or facilities, incorporating in-person visits, tailored programming, and hands-on meal preparation guidance.

---

Digital Assets and Brand Visual Identity

Brand visual identity and web asset preparation were assessed alongside offer development. Website design discussions evaluated color palettes comprising orange, white, yellow, and pink, leading to a concrete design directive to replace orange interface elements and buttons with pink. Color nuances were analyzed, specifically comparing "ranch" and "poppy" pink hues against potential dark or black website backgrounds. Professional photography assets provided directly by the photographer were confirmed ready to populate site templates; however, active website design was paused to prioritize completing the core business offer documentation.

---

Offer Worksheet Breakdown and Framework Critique

The session critically examines the prescriptive curriculum of the "Week Two Offer Sheet" (a thirteen-step framework originating from KBCU). Friction arose regarding proprietary terminology within the worksheet:

  • Costly Problem / Pain Points: The prompt asking for a "costly problem" was translated into standard sales terminology as addressing core customer pain points, such as clients feeling lost regarding workout routines and unable to achieve desired results.
  • Economic Value and Target Demographics: Quantifying the economic value serves to calculate the financial cost of unsolved health problems. Conversely, the "Who is this not for" section explicitly screens out individuals unwilling to commit the required time and financial investment.
  • Market Claims and Psychological Drivers: Complex worksheet prompts concerning "claims about the market," customer frustrations, fears of injury or failure, and the "magic model" (result, condition, barrier, and enemy) were critiqued as overly complicated jargon for straightforward training services.
  • Tiered Offer Architecture: The structure requires defining a Core offer that independently fulfills the primary promise, paired with a Premium tier (such as a $1,900-per-month package or a $22,000 annual upfront agreement) that sells direct access and elevated proximity rather than fixing a deficient core service.

---

Brick-and-Mortar Acquisition vs. Service Scaling

As an alternative to scaling independent digital and concierge training services, the participants explored physical gym ownership and facility management. Speaker 1 expressed a preference for operating an existing brick-and-mortar facility, specifically referencing Gabby Walker's gym. Discussion points included:

  • Asset Valuation: The physical facility's equipment investment exceeded $100,000, which Lance evaluated as an accessible, standard capital threshold for an established business.
  • Management Arrangement: Rather than funding a full buyout of the equipment, Lance proposed negotiating an operational arrangement where Speaker 1 takes over management of the gym while targeting an owner compensation or operational draw of $5,000 to $6,000 per month.

---

AI Automation and Tool Delegation Strategy

To overcome the friction of manually filling out the extensive thirteen-step framework under tight deadlines, the participants decided to delegate worksheet completion to an AI assistant named Biscuit. With Speaker 1 falling behind on preparation for Hunter's upcoming weekend course in San Diego, the strategy shifted to loading the raw "Week Two Offer Sheet" directly into Biscuit via voice prompts. The objective is to let the AI process the unformatted brainstorming inputs, condense messaging, and populate the required curriculum fields rapidly.
