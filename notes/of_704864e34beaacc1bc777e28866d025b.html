Meeting Notes

Competitive Landscape and Brand Philosophy

  • A comparison was made between Tesla and German automakers, particularly BMW.
  • Tesla is seen as taking a more aggressive, risk-tolerant, “deeply American” approach, enabling rapid experimentation with features like “Full Self-Driving.”
  • German manufacturers, like BMW, are viewed as more cautious, politically correct, and constrained by a fear of mistakes.
  • Despite this, BMW’s technology was described as superior, though the brand’s mentality is considered “ten years behind.”
  • This disconnect is attributed to encountering “dork fucking MBA motherfuckers” in the U.S. rather than the engineers who invent the cars.

Internal Process and System Failures

  • A new ordering system launched in July was called a “fuck up” because it was designed by people who have never sold a car.
  • A major discussion focused on service department issues, specifically a differential fluid problem common in Audi and present in their own vehicles.
  • Technicians and service advisors are reportedly failing to address this issue on in-warranty cars unless customers explicitly complain.
  • Reasons for this failure include:

    • - Service advisors not proactively asking about symptoms like vibrations or noises. - Technicians avoiding the job because the pay does not match the time required.

  • This practice results in customer dissatisfaction, degraded vehicle performance, and higher costs for the used car department when the issue is found post-warranty.

Product and Inventory Discussion

  • The group agrees they currently have the “best new cars,” specifically praising the new X5.
  • Current inventory includes three six-cylinder and one V8 old-body X5s; the V8 models are slow-moving.
  • Upcoming Audi models (Q7 and Q9) were cited as potential competitors, with the Q9 expected to be “a little better than the X7.”
  • The group discussed X7 seat configurations, debating bench seats (fold flat) versus bucket seats (pass-through).
  • Ventilated seats will be available in the new X3, addressing a previous gap versus competitors like the Nissan Rogue.

Sales Performance and Service Metrics

  • The service department is missing its daily customer-pay parts sales target of $7,400 by about $200–$400.
  • The department is not overbooked; appointments are readily available, which is considered positive for customer service.
  • “Customer pay” work includes any non-warranty work and services paid via maintenance plans.
  • Work under maintenance plans pays less than direct customer-paid work.

Electric Vehicle (EV) Market and Strategy

  • The iX3 is expected to start around $55,000, higher than a base Tesla Model 3 at $42,000.
  • Tesla’s lease prices are highly competitive, with a Model 3 as low as $299 per month.
  • BMW’s marketing is heavily focused on the iX3, with the first few months of production already sold out.
  • BMW has adopted cylindrical cell battery technology, similar to Tesla, improving range and charging speed.
