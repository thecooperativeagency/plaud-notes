09-24 Reasoning Note: Scaling a CRM Replacement Dashboard and Addressing Unbudgeted Compute Costs

id of_f682c3beb42f8e6960542e1aff46508dinfer

[](plaud://image?type=summaryCard&id=oc_su_f682c3beb42f8e6960542e1aff46508d_i1)

Core Synopsis

The engineering lead has successfully developed a prototype dashboard that fully replicates the core data-mining functionality of the dealership's former CRM, Mastermind. This new tool integrates the entire customer portfolio from the DMS, allowing salespeople to filter by metrics like contract maturity dates, which immediately provides a list of actionable sales leads. While the initial feedback from the sales team is overwhelmingly positive, confirming the tool could replace Mastermind and save that expense, the primary strategic bottleneck is now cost-scaling. The lead developer is concerned that expanding from a single-instance pilot to individual dashboards for every salesperson will incur significant, unbudgeted compute costs, potentially several hundred dollars per month. This transforms the project from a personal development initiative into a formal product with financial implications that need to be addressed before a full rollout.

The Pilot Program: A "Mastermind" Replacement

1. The Problem Gap

  • Status Quo: The dealership cancelled its subscription to the Mastermind CRM, leaving salespeople without a tool to mine the existing customer base for new opportunities (e.g., upcoming lease maturities).
  • The Pain: Salespeople expressed frustration over losing this capability and requested that management bring Mastermind back.
  • The Critical Need: A simple, effective way for the sales team to identify and engage high-potential customers within the dealership's portfolio without incurring the cost of a full-featured CRM.

2. The Solution: An Integrated Dashboard

  • Core Functionality: The new tool is a standalone dashboard, not a full CRM. It has ingested the entire customer portfolio from the DMS, creating a complete analog of the dealership's business history.
  • Key Feature: A "Portfolio Mode" allows users to apply dynamic filters, such as "maturities in the next month," to instantly generate lists of customers with upcoming service or purchasing needs. This directly replicates the "mining" function that salespeople valued in Mastermind.
  • Data Integrity: The dashboard includes historical sales data, such as which salesperson originally sold the car, but it intentionally excludes new vehicle notes, as that feature was deemed low-value "silliness" by the developer.

The Strategic Crossroads: Scaling and Cost

1. The Scaling Request

The immediate user request is to provide each salesperson with their own personalized dashboard view, pre-filtered to their specific customer list. This would allow them to see their maturing contracts and other opportunities without having to manually select their name.

2. The Bottleneck: Unbudgeted Compute Costs

  • Thesis (The Pilot): In its current state, the tool is a low-cost personal project developed by the lead.
  • Antithesis (The Rollout): Deploying individualized dashboards for the entire sales team would dramatically increase server activity ("compute"). This would shift the project from a negligible expense to a significant operational cost, estimated at a potential $400-$600 per month.
  • Synthesis (The Decision Point): The developer believes creating individual dashboards is technically "doable" since it uses the same underlying data. However, a decision must be made on whether the business is willing to fund these new operational costs to fully replace Mastermind.
